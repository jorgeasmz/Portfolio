## The Metric That Nearly Hid a Failure

The German Credit dataset ships something most datasets do not: an explicit cost matrix.
Approving an applicant who defaults costs five times what rejecting a creditworthy one does.
That asymmetry is the whole problem, and accuracy is blind to it.

I built the obvious model first, a Random Forest, and it scored **0.735 accuracy** on a held-out
stratified split. That looks reasonable next to the 0.700 you get by predicting that nobody
defaults. Under the cost matrix it scores **153**, and rejecting every single applicant scores
**140**.

The model was worse than refusing to lend at all, and the accuracy figure gave no hint of it.

## Choosing by Cost

Evaluated properly, against baselines and on the metric the domain actually cares about:

| Model | Accuracy | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | Recall | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Logistic Regression** | 0.750 | **0.806** | **0.633** | 0.800 | **98** |
| Random Forest | 0.735 | 0.788 | 0.615 | 0.583 | 153 |
| Majority class | 0.700 | 0.500 | 0.300 | 0.000 | 300 |

The linear model wins on every measure. On 800 training rows of mostly categorical data that is
not surprising: there is not enough signal for an ensemble to exploit, and there is plenty for it
to overfit. Its coefficients also carry a practical advantage that a forest cannot match, since a
declined application has to be explainable.

## The Threshold Is a Decision, Not a Default

`predict()` cuts at 0.5, which silently assumes both kinds of error cost the same. Sweeping the
threshold against the cost curve moves the optimum to **0.45**, bringing expected cost down to
**96**. Past that point accuracy keeps rising while cost gets worse: the most accurate threshold
in the sweep costs 25% more than the chosen one.

## Architecture

Preprocessing and classifier live inside a single scikit-learn `Pipeline`. That is a deliberate
choice rather than a stylistic one: imputation, scaling and one-hot encoding are fitted as part
of the estimator, so the API accepts raw application fields and there is no second implementation
of the transformations that could drift out of sync with training.

The service runs as a **FastAPI** container on Render, declared in a `render.yaml` blueprint,
with a **Streamlit** client on Streamlit Cloud reaching it over HTTP. The two halves are joined
by a single environment variable, which is what lets them be deployed and scaled independently.

## Engineering Notes

The repository carries 23 tests at 97% coverage, a CI workflow running lint and tests, and pinned
dependencies. Two of those tests exist because of specific defects found while building it: the
OpenAPI example never reached Swagger because of a typo in the schema config, and a Compose
volume was hiding the model the image had trained, leaving a fresh clone answering 503 to every
request.
