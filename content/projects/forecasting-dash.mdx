## Series

Seven series with different structure, stored in PostgreSQL alongside the parameters used
to model each one.

| Series | Observations | Frequency | Cycle | Horizon |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| Airline passengers | 144 | monthly | 12 | 12 |
| Monthly car sales | 108 | monthly | 12 | 12 |
| Monthly sunspots | 600 | monthly | 1 | 12 |
| Daily minimum temperatures | 1,460 | daily | 365 | 28 |
| Daily female births | 365 | daily | 7 | 14 |
| Colombian spot price, daily | 3,896 | daily | 7 | 14 |
| Colombian spot price, hourly | 93,504 | hourly | 24 | 24 |

The first five are published CSVs. The last two are the same measurement at two grains,
read from `marts.fct_system_hourly` in the warehouse of a separate project, whose columns
and types are under an enforced contract there. A column that changes type fails that
project's build rather than this project's ingestion.

The read happens once, when a series is ingested, and the observations are stored here
like any other. Nothing the API or the dashboard serves reaches the warehouse, so this
demo stays up while that database is asleep and a backtest refits without a network round
trip per fold. Aggregation to the daily grain runs in the warehouse rather than here, so
that series transfers 3,896 rows instead of 93,504 to compute the same means, and a day
missing an hour is dropped rather than averaged over fewer values than the rest.

## Fitting cost at the hourly grain

A fold refits every family, so the length of the training set decides whether a family can
be compared at all. One fit at a seasonal period of 24:

| Training observations | seasonal_naive | lightgbm | prophet | sarima |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2,184 | 0.0 s | 0.4 s | 0.6 s | 18.1 s |
| 8,760 | 0.0 s | 0.5 s | 3.8 s | 96.6 s |
| 26,280 | 0.0 s | 0.9 s | 11.9 s | **over 420 s** |
| 93,504 | 0.0 s | 1.4 s | 39.8 s | not attempted |

SARIMA is the constraint. Its cost grows faster than the series does, and past 26,280
observations a single fit exceeds seven minutes, which four folds cannot afford. The other
three are unaffected: LightGBM fits the whole history in 1.4 s.

The hourly series therefore trains each fold on the most recent 8,760 observations rather
than on everything before the cutoff. That is one year, it holds every fold to the same
size, and it keeps all four families in the comparison at about four minutes for the
series.

## Results

Lowest mean MASE per series across four folds:

| Series | Selected model | MASE | Coverage |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Colombian spot price, daily | sarima | 0.621 | 91% |
| Daily minimum temperatures | prophet | 0.688 | 75% |
| Daily female births | prophet | 0.779 | 82% |
| Airline passengers | sarima | 0.794 | 67% |
| Monthly car sales | seasonal_naive | 0.999 | 100% |
| Colombian spot price, hourly | seasonal_naive | 1.209 | 100% |
| Monthly sunspots | lightgbm | 2.041 | 35% |

All four families win at least one series. On two of the seven no model reaches the
in-sample scale of the seasonal naive, and on two the baseline itself is the best forecast.

The first row is not comparable to the rest, for the reason given under the training window below.

## The hourly spot price

Nothing improves on the baseline.

| Model | MASE | MAE | Coverage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **seasonal_naive** | **1.209** | **66.15** | 100% |
| sarima | 1.210 | 66.23 | 86% |
| prophet | 1.335 | 73.06 | 92% |
| lightgbm | 1.553 | 84.97 | 50% |

SARIMA and the baseline are separated by 0.001, which is a tie rather than a ranking, and
the two families that model a trend are both worse. The same hour of the previous day is a
strong forecast of this one, and a day-ahead horizon is short enough that little else is
left to predict.

## Training window and the MASE denominator

The daily series produced the largest result here, and it is a result about the metric
rather than about the market.

Over the decade the warehouse holds, the level is not stationary: values run from 61.2 to
2,498.8 and the yearly means from 106 in 2017 to 676 in 2024. Fitted through all of it
with an expanding window, Prophet extrapolates a linear trend that lands where the series
does not go. Capping every fold at the most recent three years is the same code with one
number changed.

| Model | MAE, expanding | MAE, 1,095 days | MASE, expanding | MASE, 1,095 days |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| sarima | 85.53 | **82.06** | 1.199 | **0.621** |
| seasonal_naive | 108.13 | **108.13** | 1.515 | **0.817** |
| prophet | 690.62 | **126.30** | 9.679 | **0.955** |
| lightgbm | 147.58 | **145.12** | 2.070 | **1.096** |

Only one of those rows describes a model that improved. Prophet's absolute error falls by
more than a factor of five and its interval coverage goes from 0% to 96%, which is the
failure being fixed: 0% coverage is a stronger statement than a wide interval, since not
one held-out day fell inside the band. The other three barely move. SARIMA gains 4%,
LightGBM 2%, and the seasonal naive reports the same 108.13 twice.

Every MASE nonetheless falls by roughly half, the seasonal naive's included. Its forecasts
did not change and could not have: it reads the seven observations before the cutoff, which
the window does not touch. What changed is the denominator. MASE divides by the in-sample
error of the seasonal naive over the training set, and over three volatile recent years
that error is about 1.85 times what it is over the whole decade, so every ratio built on it
shrinks.

**MASE is scale-free across series, not across training windows within one series.** A
figure of 0.621 here and 0.688 on the temperature series are not the same kind of number,
because the two denominators are computed over different spans. Model selection is
unaffected, since the denominator is computed once per fold and shared by every family,
which is why the ranking by MASE and the ranking by MAE agree in both columns above and why
SARIMA is selected either way.

The window was chosen by trying three values against the same four folds the table reports,
so 0.621 is optimistic in the way any figure is when the configuration behind it was picked
on the data it is measured against. Isolating that would need a validation split held out
from these folds, which at four folds per series this project does not have.

## Car sales

On this series no family improves on the seasonal naive:

| Model | MASE | MAE |
|---|---:|---:|
| **seasonal_naive** | **0.999** | **1,584.98** |
| prophet | 1.114 | 1,778.63 |
| lightgbm | 1.130 | 1,801.69 |
| sarima | 1.306 | 2,086.20 |

Measured on the final twelve months alone the ordering inverts: Prophet leads at 0.866 and
the seasonal naive comes last at 1.270. Averaging over four folds is what separates a model
from a favourable window.

## Interval calibration

Nominal interval width is 80%. Observed coverage across models and series:

| Model | Coverage range |
|---|---|
| sarima | 56% to 91% |
| seasonal_naive | 71% to 100% |
| prophet | 40% to 96% |
| lightgbm | 35% to 55% |

LightGBM under-covers on every series. Its intervals come from quantile regression at 0.1
and 0.9 evaluated at each recursive step, and that procedure does not accumulate the
uncertainty introduced by feeding predicted values back into the lag features. SARIMA,
whose intervals derive from the state-space model variance, tracks the nominal level most
closely.

## Choice of error metric

The series span four orders of magnitude, from single-digit temperatures to car sales in
the tens of thousands, and two of them contain values below 1.0: sunspots reach 0.20 and
temperatures 0.50. A five-unit error against an observation of 0.20 registers as 2,500%,
which would dominate any average containing it. MASE divides by the in-sample mean absolute
error of the seasonal naive, giving a figure where 1.0 matches that baseline, with the
qualification above about what happens when the training set is windowed.

## Architecture

Ingestion and backtesting are a batch job that writes to the database; the dashboard and
the API read from it. Two sources feed ingestion, published CSVs over HTTP and the energy
warehouse over PostgreSQL, and both arrive as the same two columns before anything else
sees them.

Series metadata travels with the stored series rather than being read from the source
registry at serving time, so both consumers depend on the database alone. The training
window is part of that metadata: fitting on the full history at serving time would serve a
model whose accuracy nothing measured, so the same window the backtest scored is the one
the served fit uses.

The batch job accepts a single series, which is what makes rescoring one of them a
thirty-second operation rather than a refetch of 93,504 rows and a refit of the other six.

## Testing

79 tests at 94% coverage of the application packages. The suite runs offline: series are
read from fixtures rather than downloaded, the warehouse is never connected to, models are
fitted on a 60-point synthetic series, and the database is a temporary SQLite file. CI
additionally applies the migrations against a PostgreSQL service container.

Which fold trained on how much is not observable from outside the backtest, and it is the
property the training window exists to control, so a stand-in model family records the size
of every frame it is fitted on and the tests assert the window from that.
