## Corpus scope

The corpus is 240 arXiv papers on quantum key distribution and quantum cryptography rather than
a sample of `quant-ph` as a whole. Retrieval across a category of 185,000 papers on unrelated
subjects mostly measures topic separation, since almost any method tells an optics paper from an
error-correction one. Within one field that signal is absent, and what remains measured is
passage selection.

The difference is large enough to reverse a design decision. On a general `quant-ph` corpus of
249 papers the same question set reaches 0.82 passage accuracy with no reranking; within quantum
cryptography the same configuration reaches 0.43.

Papers are ingested from their LaTeX source rather than from the rendered PDF. A two-column
preprint loses in layout what a citation needs: section headings become indistinguishable from
body text and paragraphs fragment around displayed equations. Measured on the first three papers,
PDF extraction recovered one section per paper, the title, while the LaTeX source recovers the
hierarchy the document declares.

| | |
|---|---:|
| Papers ingested | 240 |
| Chunks | 18,969 |
| Distinct sections | 2,952 |
| Mean chunk length | 530 characters |

Of 300 papers requested, 60 were dropped: 47 whose source the LaTeX renderer could not process
and the rest without retrievable source. That 20% is a property of what authors upload rather
than of the pipeline.

## Retriever comparison

Nineteen hand-written questions, fourteen of them anchored to the passage they were written from.
A result is judged twice: whether the answering paper appears in the top five, and whether that
passage does.

| Retriever | Paper found | Passage found | MRR | Latency ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `postgres fts` | 0.37 | 0.07 | 0.215 | 95 |
| `dense` | 0.58 | 0.43 | 0.342 | 11 |
| `bm25` | 0.84 | 0.43 | 0.627 | **4** |
| `hybrid` | 0.79 | 0.43 | 0.636 | 24 |
| **`hybrid+rerank`** | **0.89** | **0.71** | **0.718** | 1,108 |

Fusion combines ranks rather than scores. A BM25 score and a cosine similarity live on scales
that are not comparable and neither is calibrated, so weighting them directly would require
normalising a quantity with no fixed range.

The same code is measured against BEIR SciFact, whose relevance judgements make the figures
comparable to published ones. There `bm25` reaches nDCG@10 0.652 and `dense` 0.702, which is
where the published SciFact numbers for those methods sit. That is the check that the
implementation is correct rather than merely self-consistent. Serving both encoders as int8
graphs costs 0.011 of nDCG on `dense` and `hybrid` and 0.006 with reranking, and BM25 does not
move, which is the check that the difference comes from the encoders rather than from the
harness.

## Term index storage

BM25 is built in process from the stored chunks as one compressed sparse matrix of term
frequencies rather than as a dictionary per document. The scoring is the Okapi formula either
way and both retrieve the same passages, so the storage is what decides whether the index fits
in a service or dominates it.

| | Resident | Query |
|---|---:|---:|
| Dictionary per document | 558 MB | 86 ms |
| **Sparse matrix** | **44 MB** | **4 ms** |

The matrix itself holds 8.6 MB of the 44. The two differ in how they treat a term common enough
to earn a negative weight under the unsmoothed formula: the library floors it, this
implementation smooths it, and the ordering inside the top five shifts accordingly.

Moving lexical search into Postgres was measured as the alternative that needs no index in the
process at all. `ts_rank_cd` carries no inverse document frequency, so a term appearing in most
passages weighs as much as one appearing in two, and the ranking reaches 0.07 passage accuracy
against 0.43 for BM25.

## Reranking depth

| Depth | Paper found | Passage found | MRR | Retrieval p50 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No reranking | 0.79 | 0.43 | 0.636 | 24 ms |
| 5 | 0.79 | 0.43 | 0.605 | 384 ms |
| 10 | 0.89 | 0.57 | **0.732** | 1,248 ms |
| **25** | **0.89** | **0.71** | 0.718 | 3,762 ms |
| 50 | 0.84 | 0.71 | 0.673 | |

Depth 5 costs 360 ms and recovers nothing the fusion had not already ranked. Depth 10 reaches
the best MRR at a third of the latency. Depth 25 is what ships, because passage selection is the
operation the service performs and 0.71 against 0.57 is two more of the fourteen anchored
questions, on a request whose generation step takes nine seconds regardless. Those latencies
are measured with the cross-encoder scoring 25 pairs per forward pass; scoring two per pass,
which the memory budget below settles on, reaches 1,108 ms at the same depth.

The two evaluations disagree about the reranker. On 300 judged SciFact queries the cross-encoder
lowers nDCG@10 from 0.709 to 0.703 and reducing the depth does not recover the loss, which is
consistent with SciFact being claim verification against abstracts and the model being trained
on web passages. On the corpus it raises passage selection from 0.43 to 0.71. It is enabled on
the second basis, with the caveat that fourteen anchored questions is a small sample. A larger
cross-encoder is not an alternative at this budget: measured over five queries at the same
depth, `bge-reranker-base` takes 19.1 s per query against 3.2 s for the one in use.

## Answer construction

A comparison is not one retrieval. Retrieving once for a question about how two protocols differ
returns passages about whichever side dominates the query, so the graph fans out and retrieves
per side before answering. Routing is a regular expression rather than a model call, since the
free tier meters requests and one spent classifying is one not spent answering.

Every claim carries a bracketed number naming the passage it came from, and every number is
resolved back to a paper, a section and a paragraph after the answer is written. A number outside
what was retrieved is reported as a fabrication rather than rendered as a citation.

The provider holds a chain of models. A 429 means that model's daily quota is spent, which no
amount of waiting fixes, so the next model is tried immediately; a 503 means it is briefly busy,
which waiting does fix.

## Grounding

Twenty-five questions: the nineteen the corpus covers, and six on subjects it does not contain
at all.

| | Covered | Uncovered |
|---|---:|---:|
| Questions | 19 | 6 |
| Fabricated citations | **0** | **0** |
| Declined to answer | 6 | **6** |
| Sentences carrying a citation | 0.98 | |
| Citations per answer | 1.7 | |

A fabricated citation is a bracketed number naming a passage that was never retrieved. There
were none on either set, and no uncovered question was answered.

The six refusals among covered questions are consistent with the passage accuracy above: 6 of 19
is 32%, against the 29% of anchored questions whose passage the retriever misses. Retrieval
failure surfaces as a refusal rather than as a plausible sentence.

The schema is what makes a citation resolvable. A citation names a paper, a paragraph and the
piece within it, and that triple carries a unique constraint. The arXiv identifier keeps its
version suffix, so a revised paper is a distinct row rather than a silent overwrite of the text
a stored citation points at. Long paragraphs are split on sentence boundaries with one sentence
of overlap, and every piece keeps the index of the paragraph it came from.

## Semantic cache calibration

A cache hit does not save a millisecond, it saves one of the twenty daily requests the free tier
allows per model. Matching is by embedding similarity, and the measurement says that separates
the two classes poorly within one field.

| Pairs | Similarity |
|---|---|
| The 171 pairs of the question set | 0.496 to 0.723 |
| Five paraphrases of set questions | 0.766 to 0.974 |
| An adjacent pair asking different things | **0.823** |
| One of those paraphrases | **0.733** |

The last two rows are the finding. A pair asking how key rates are computed for two different
protocol families scores above a genuine paraphrase. Questions in one field share vocabulary, and
the embedding is dominated by what a question is about rather than by what it asks, so the
classes overlap and no threshold separates them.

The two errors are not symmetric. A miss spends a request; a false hit answers a different
question and attaches to it the passages retrieved for the question actually asked. The threshold
is therefore 0.90, above both measured populations, which admits close rewordings and rejects
paraphrases that change the vocabulary. Calibrating instead against negatives drawn from
unrelated fields puts the threshold near 0.70 and admits the 0.823 pair.

## Memory budget

The free tier the service runs on allows 512 MB. Three measured changes place it at 265 MB under
sustained load.

| | Resident | Startup |
|---|---:|---:|
| Library term index, encoders under PyTorch | 1,177 MB | 35.8 s |
| Term index as a sparse matrix | 479 MB | |
| Encoders as ONNX graphs | 457 MB | 2.3 s |
| Encoders quantised to int8 | 275 MB | **1.6 s** |
| **Sustained load, arena off, 2 pairs per pass** | **265 MB** | |

A resident set sampled between requests is not the budget. ONNX Runtime allocates from an arena
that retains every block it takes and asks for a new one per tensor shape, so a service seeing
questions of varying length grows to 1,264 MB under load. Fixed padding widths bound that growth,
which is what they are for, but only at 1.4 GB, and coarse ones cost compute: rounding a
320-token batch to 512 raised the resident set to 2,999 MB and retrieval to 5,260 ms.

With the arena disabled, resident sits near 265 MB and the transient allocation during one
request is governed by how many pairs the cross-encoder scores per forward pass.

| Pairs per pass | Resident | Peak | Retrieval p50 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 25 | 275 MB | 836 MB | 3,762 ms |
| 8 | 266 MB | 458 MB | 2,539 ms |
| 4 | 255 MB | 341 MB | 1,974 ms |
| **2** | 265 MB | **372 MB** | **1,108 ms** |

Scoring is independent per pair, so the number in a forward pass changes nothing about the
result. Smaller passes are also faster here, since they fit the cache better than one large pass.
The answer path adds the model client and the streaming machinery on top of the retrieval path,
at 326 MB with two pairs per pass against 455 MB with four.

## Serving

There is no deep learning framework in the serving image. The encoders are exported graphs and
tokenisation goes through the Rust tokenizer, so the inference stack is `onnxruntime` and
`tokenizers`, and imports cost 100 MB rather than 434. `torch` writes the graphs and never runs
them, which is why the image builds in two stages and the first is discarded.

Pooling and normalisation are inside the exported graph rather than reimplemented at call time.
Both have to agree exactly with what the model was trained under, and a graph carrying them
cannot drift from a reimplementation. Parity was checked against the original embeddings at
cosine 1.0 and identical reranker logits.

`POST /search` performs retrieval, reaches no model and consumes no quota. `GET /answer` streams
server-sent events: the passages first, then the answer, then its resolved citations, so a reader
sees what the answer is drawn from while it is being written.

Generating an answer needs a key, which the caller sends in `X-Api-Key`. The free tier allows
twenty requests per model per day, so a public demo answering with the host's key would be spent
by early afternoon. Retrieval stays open because it reaches no model at all, and a key configured
on the server is used when the header is absent.

## Deployment

| Component | Host |
|---|---|
| API | Render |
| Corpus | Neon, Postgres with `pgvector` |
| Client | Vercel |

The corpus is moved rather than rebuilt, since re-ingesting means fetching three hundred sources
from arXiv again at the three-second delay the API asks between requests. The local database is
dumped and restored instead.

Both the migration and the restore go through Neon's direct endpoint rather than the pooled one.
`pg_dump` opens its script with a session-level `set_config('search_path', '', false)`, and a
pooler working in transaction mode hands the same server connection to the next client, which
inherits that empty search path and cannot resolve an unqualified table name. The application
uses the pooled endpoint, where it issues only ordinary queries.

The client is a Next.js page over the same two endpoints. Citations in the answer are controls
that reveal the passage they name, and a citation the service could not resolve is marked rather
than rendered. The visitor's key is held in browser storage, sent only with their own requests,
and read through `useSyncExternalStore` so the prerendered markup and the first client render
agree on an empty value.

## Testing

The suite contains 87 Python tests and 6 in the client. No test reaches the network, the database
or a model. The event-stream reader is separated from the component and tested against both CRLF
and LF terminators, which the specification permits and implementations differ on. CI applies the
migration against a `pgvector` service container, since the schema declares a vector column a
plain Postgres image cannot create.
