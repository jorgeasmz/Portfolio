## Failure mode of the sentiment checkpoint

A curated set of 29 cases, grouped by the linguistic phenomenon each one probes, places the
served SST-2 checkpoint at 82.8% overall and 33% on sarcasm. The sarcastic failures are not
located near the decision boundary. They return at the top of the range:

| Text | Expected | Predicted | Score |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Oh brilliant, another update that breaks everything. | NEGATIVE | POSITIVE | 1.00 |
| Wonderful, it arrived in three pieces. Exactly what I wanted. | NEGATIVE | POSITIVE | 1.00 |
| Never again. | NEGATIVE | POSITIVE | 0.99 |

A sentence-level classifier reads surface lexical cues, and irony inverts the intended polarity
while leaving those cues intact. Discarding low-confidence predictions therefore retains the
inverted ones.

The extent of that is measurable. A logistic regression fitted on the checkpoint's own output,
a single feature holding its confidence signed by polarity, recovers irony on the TweetEval test
split at an ROC-AUC of **0.479**. The output carries no information about whether its input is
ironic, which places the failure outside the reach of any threshold on the score and makes a
second classifier the available response.

## Baselines and fine-tuning

The irony head is fine-tuned from `distilbert-base-uncased` on the TweetEval irony
configuration: 2,862 training tweets, 955 validation and 784 held out, the last at 39.7% ironic.
Every row below decides at argmax, the rule the published benchmark numbers use.

| Model | Accuracy | Macro F1 | F1 ironic | Precision | Recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sentiment probe | 0.523 | 0.467 | **0.294** | 0.356 | 0.251 |
| Majority class | 0.397 | 0.284 | **0.568** | 0.397 | 1.000 |
| TF-IDF and logistic regression | 0.667 | 0.659 | **0.605** | 0.571 | 0.643 |
| Fine-tuned, four blocks frozen | 0.663 | 0.660 | **0.624** | 0.560 | 0.704 |
| Fine-tuned, all parameters | 0.672 | 0.672 | **0.664** | 0.559 | 0.817 |

The majority class predicts the ironic label, which the training split favours, and reaches
perfect recall with no discrimination. Word and character n-grams into logistic regression
measure what surface features recover without a language model. Freezing the embeddings and the
lowest four transformer blocks leaves 14.8M of 67.0M parameters trainable and costs 0.040 of
ironic-class F1. Full fine-tuning is the run that is exported and served.

Each run is recorded in MLflow with its hyperparameters, per-epoch metrics, held-out metrics and
confusion matrix, on a SQLite backend since MLflow 3 places the filesystem store in maintenance
mode.

## Decision threshold

| Operating point | Threshold | Accuracy | Macro F1 | F1 ironic | Precision | Recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Argmax | 0.50 | 0.675 | **0.672** | 0.706 | 0.622 | 0.818 |
| Selected | 0.84 | 0.707 | **0.706** | 0.688 | 0.699 | 0.678 |

The threshold is selected on the validation split and written to `decision.json` beside the
weights, so the operating point is an output of training rather than a constant in the serving
code.

The selection criterion is macro F1 rather than ironic-class F1. Both error directions carry
cost for a flag: an undetected ironic input leaves an inverted sentiment label unqualified, and
a false alarm on plain text makes the flag uninformative. Ironic-class F1 prices only the first,
and selecting on it places the threshold at 0.44, where the head flags 25 of the 29 curated
cases rather than 17.

The selection also runs on the exported graph rather than on the checkpoint. Quantisation shifts
the probability scale, so the same criterion resolves to 0.89 on the checkpoint and 0.84 on the
int8 graph. Selecting on the artifact that is served keeps the threshold and the scores it is
compared against on one scale.

## Runtime selection

Each runtime is held to one intra-op thread, which approximates a container on a free tier, and
decides at argmax. Scoring them against the selected threshold would credit quantisation for the
fact that the threshold was chosen on the quantised graph.

| Runtime | Size MB | p50 ms | p95 ms | Accuracy | F1 ironic |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| PyTorch fp32 | 268 | 67.3 | 87.6 | 0.672 | 0.664 |
| ONNX fp32 | 269 | 35.2 | 51.7 | 0.672 | 0.664 |
| **ONNX int8** | **68** | **15.8** | **25.3** | 0.667 | 0.658 |

Exporting the graph halves the latency at identical weights, and quantising the weights to int8
halves it again while reducing the artifact from 268 MB to 68 MB, for 0.006 of ironic-class F1.
The int8 graph is the one served, since on a free tier the artifact size governs image pull time
and cold-start latency.

The ONNX exporter records shapes for intermediate tensors and then optimises the graph without
updating them. The quantiser re-runs shape inference and rejects the contradiction, so the stale
entries are removed before quantisation and recomputed from the graph.

## Domain transfer

The curated set consists of formal written English, which is outside the domain the head was
fitted on. At the selected threshold it flags 17 of the 29 cases, including 5 of the 6 plainly
negative ones, so the score distribution shifts upward and separates the classes poorly on that
text.

The held-out TweetEval figures are the ones that characterise the head. On tweets the two
classes separate: the median ironic score is 0.944 against 0.402 for non-ironic. A deployment on
text unlike a tweet would require its threshold selected on a sample of that text.

## Serving

`core` owns the models, `api` owns HTTP, `web` owns presentation, and `training` owns everything
that produces an artifact rather than serving one. The training package is absent from the
serving image, since a trainer, a dataset loader and a tracking server add layers that never
answer a request.

Both heads are loaded once behind a lock, since FastAPI serves synchronous endpoints from a
worker threadpool and two concurrent first requests would otherwise each begin loading the same
checkpoint. Every response carries an irony verdict, so readiness covers both heads rather than
reporting them separately, and a failed load leaves the process alive with `model_ready` false.

Each head truncates at the width it was fitted on, 512 tokens for sentiment and 96 for irony,
and a test asserts that the serving constant and the training constant agree.

## Deployment

The API runs as a Docker Space on Hugging Face and the Streamlit client on Streamlit Cloud,
joined by one environment variable. The irony head is in neither repository: it is published as a
Hugging Face model repository carrying the int8 graph, the tokenizer and `decision.json`, and the
loader resolves it at startup. The model card is generated from the tracked run, so the
documented metrics and the recorded run cannot diverge.

A free Space suspends on inactivity and needs time to wake and load both heads, so the client
allows 90 seconds and reports a timeout explicitly.

## Testing

The suite contains 40 tests at 96% statement coverage of the application and model packages. No
test downloads a model: each drives a fake pipeline or a fake ONNX session and asserts on how it
was called, including that undeclared tensors are dropped from the feed and that inputs are cast
to the int64 the exported graph declares.
